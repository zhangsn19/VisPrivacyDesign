# From Design Proposals to Six Concepts

## Inputs and synthesis procedure

The first phase used the design proposal guide to elicit open-ended design ideas. Participants selected their own visual privacy scenario and submitted at least two ideas with descriptions and optional sketches or screenshots. The current manuscript reports 13 participants and does not restrict the target device or platform to smart glasses.

The manuscript describes the synthesis as iterative affinity diagramming. Two researchers extracted core features from the design artifacts into digital notes, grouped functionally and visually similar features, and refined the groups through consensus discussions. The resulting groups informed both the design-space dimensions and six representative design concepts. The manuscript separately describes thematic analysis of the explanations accompanying the designs; this document does not fill in its unresolved coding details.

The six concepts therefore represent a synthesis across proposals. They should not be described as six participant submissions chosen unchanged.

## What was compared

The available proposal-extraction worksheet records target users, devices, timing, visual content, presentation form, integration with the scene, explanation style, and user actions. These categories make the transition from open-ended answers to interface concepts understandable.

| Feature in the extraction worksheet | Examples in the available rows | Relationship to the manuscript's design space |
|---|---|---|
| Presentation and integration with the scene | Overlays, side panels, independent windows; high, medium, or low scene integration | Helps distinguish object-grounded and interface-grounded visual anchors |
| Timing | Real-time and post-event presentations | Supports the distinction between in-situ and post-situ delivery |
| Explanation | Text, natural-language summaries, visual inference graphs | Provides material for comparing explanatory depth; final depth labels require an interpretive coding decision |
| User actions | Masking, allowing or refusing inference, deleting inferences, managing memory and use boundaries | Informs the controls instantiated in the six concepts |

These examples are English summaries of categories and values in the local worksheet. The worksheet contains 15 populated proposal rows and is a partial record; this table does not assert complete coverage of all participants or a one-to-one derivation of any D1–D6 concept.

## Resulting concepts

| Concept | Main presentation and interaction |
|---|---|
| D1. Central Risk List | Consolidated review in a separate panel, with evidence and controls |
| D2. Conversational Control | Follow-up dialogue about evidence, inferences, and their use |
| D3. Marked Clues with Side Explanation | Object markers paired with an explanatory side panel |
| D4. Step-by-Step Timeline | Temporally linked evidence with controls over contributing clues |
| D5. Contextual Lightweight Hint | Brief contextual cue with details and actions on demand |
| D6. In-place Risk Action | Explanation and actions inside the relevant scene region |

The complete English narratives and existing images are in [Six Design Concepts](Six_Design_Concepts.md). This summary follows the manuscript's synthesis account and the available feature table; it is not a reconstructed researcher codebook.
