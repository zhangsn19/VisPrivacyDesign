# Evaluation Procedure

This working description combines the current manuscript's high-level procedure with explicit instructions in the available local prototype. Details specific to that source snapshot are identified below. The background instruments recorded in the later collection export are listed in [Evaluation Questionnaire](Evaluation_Questionnaire.md).

## Participant-facing sequence

1. Review the study information and provide informed consent. The manuscript describes voluntary participation and the right to quit, ask questions, or request deletion of data.
2. Read the introduction to inferential privacy and its illustrative example. Distinguish an inferred attribute from the direct recognition of a visible object.
3. View the storyboard for an assigned design and inspect its interactive prototype.
4. Open the health-related risk, inspect its evidence, and choose one of the available privacy actions. The exact two-step task below comes from the local prototype snapshot.
5. Answer the six rating items and the open question. Repeat the design-viewing and evaluation sequence for three designs.
6. Rank the three designs, explain the ranking, and confirm the choices.

The later collection export additionally records OPLIS-12, IUIPC-10, trust-in-automation responses, and checks. Recovered question text is now included in [Background Questionnaire Items](Background_Questionnaire_Items.md). Exact placement and country-specific wording still need confirmation against the administered form.

## Common interaction task in the local prototype

**Step 1. Find and open the Health status risk.** Inspect why the medicine label, follow-up reminder, and late-work time may support a health-status inference.

**Step 2. Choose an action for Health status.** Choose Address, Allow this use, or Remind me later based on your actual judgment.

The local prototype unlocks ratings after the participant opens the risk and chooses an action. A participant may choose to allow the use or defer a reminder; the task does not require everyone to choose masking.

## Shared interactive scenario

The inspected local prototype uses a desk scene. Its health-risk explanation links medicine text, follow-up details, and late-work time to a possible inference of recent treatment or chronic-condition management. Other displayed example risks concern work identity and daily routine. The desk scene used by the interaction is distinct from the varied everyday situations depicted in the six storyboards.

Risk values, inferences, and feedback are predefined interface content in this prototype. The study evaluates users' responses to the design; it does not demonstrate the effectiveness of a live privacy-protection model.

## Assignment and recruitment

The manuscript describes three randomly selected designs in a counterbalanced order and recruitment on Prolific from the U.S. and U.K., with English fluency and an approval rate of at least 95%.

The six study concepts are D1–D6. The available study reference supports three-design assignments within that set. Its exact assignment implementation remains a reference version until matched to the administered study.
