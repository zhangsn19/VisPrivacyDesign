# Speed-Dating Guide

## Procedure

1. Read the study information, provide consent, and review the introduction to inferential privacy.
2. Complete the comprehension questions about inferred information and privacy control.
3. View and try **three assigned designs from D1–D6**, one at a time. For each design, inspect the privacy risk and its evidence, then choose a privacy action.
4. Answer the six rating questions and the open question after each design.
5. Rank the three designs and explain the ranking.

The six interfaces are available in the [prototype folder](prototype/README.md).

## Per-design questionnaire

Answer based on the interaction you just completed. Choose one response from **1–5** for each item.

| Item | English wording | Response anchors |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Can you relate to the user’s privacy concern in this story? | 1 = Not at all; 5 = Completely |
| Q2 | This notification approach helps me understand what the system may infer about me | 1 = Strongly disagree; 5 = Strongly agree |
| Q3 | This notification approach makes me feel able to control privacy risks | 1 = Strongly disagree; 5 = Strongly agree |
| Q4 | This design would not interrupt the task I am currently completing | 1 = Strongly disagree; 5 = Strongly agree |
| Q5 | I would trust a system that uses this notification approach | 1 = Strongly disagree; 5 = Strongly agree |
| Q6 | Overall, this is a good notification approach | 1 = Strongly disagree; 5 = Strongly agree |

**Open response**

Why is this notification approach effective or ineffective? What situations suit it?

[Free-text response]

Complete all six ratings and the open response.


## Final ranking

Rank the three notification approaches **first, second, and third** by how effectively they help users notice and address privacy risks. Select each design once.

**Explain your ranking.**

[Free-text response]

**Confirmation:** I have reviewed and confirmed this ranking.

## Background questionnaires

### Online privacy literacy — US form

For statements, select True, False, or Unknown. For multiple-choice questions, select one answer.

**1. Data collected by social network operators are deleted after five years.**

**2. Companies combine data traces from different websites to create user profiles.**

**3. Emails commonly pass through several computers before reaching the receiver.**

**4. What is a cookie?**

- **a.** A text file that helps a website recognize a returning user
- **b.** A program that disables data collection
- **c.** A computer virus transferred by websites
- **d.** A browser plugin for safe browsing

**5. What does cache mean?**

- **a.** Buffer storage that can make browsing faster
- **b.** Software that sends user data to third parties
- **c.** Software that backs up data externally
- **d.** A plugin that encrypts browsing data

**6. What is a trojan?**

- **a.** A program disguised as useful software that performs another function
- **b.** Software that protects against malware
- **c.** A harmless program made for fun
- **d.** A virus that no longer exists

**7. The phrase “right to be left alone” is written into the U.S. Constitution.**

**8. The USA FREEDOM Act limits bulk collection of telecommunications metadata on U.S. citizens.**

**9. When subscribing to a social networking service, the company generally has the legal right to sell personal information to third parties.**

**10. Regularly deleting cookies, cache, and browsing history can make tracking internet activity more difficult.**

**11. Passwords combining letters, numbers, and symbols are generally safer than simple words or numbers.**

**12. Using different passwords for different online services helps prevent access to personal data.**
